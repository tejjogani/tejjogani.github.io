The algo trader's path

From "I understand the dashboard" to "an algorithm trades a small account while I sleep" — staged, honest, and mapped onto what you've already built.
In plain words

Algo trading is not about finding a magic entry. It's a research business: you hunt for a small, explainable edge, test it without fooling yourself, deploy it small, watch it decay, and repeat forever. The trader is the caretaker of a portfolio of edges, not the hero of any single trade. Everything below is sequenced so each stage protects you from the classic way people die at the next one.

Stage 1 — Own one explainable edge (you are here)

The bar for every strategy, forever: explain the edge in five minutes. Why does it make money? Who is on the other side, and why do they accept losing? For your current trend+regime system the answers exist and are tested: it earns the risk-transfer premium — in storms, panicked and forced sellers pay whoever will take risk off their hands; the strategy steps aside early and re-enters when they're done. If you can't give that kind of answer for a future strategy, you don't have a strategy — you have a pattern that hasn't betrayed you yet.

Exercise: write the five-minute explanation of the current system in your vault, in your own words. If you can't, reread the playbook — that gap is the curriculum.

Stage 2 — Deploy something mediocre, small

A deployed mediocre algo teaches more than ten beautiful backtests, because backtests can't show slippage, broker fills, API outages, or your own nerves. The first deployment should be the boring monthly exposure dial you already trust — paper-traded first, then tiny real size in the empty agentic account. The goal of stage 2 is not profit; it's discovering every gap between simulation and reality while the cost of discovery is pocket change.

Done when: three month-end signals have executed without you touching anything, and you have a log comparing intended vs actual fills.

Stage 3 — A portfolio of small, uncorrelated edges

One strategy is unstable — its bad month is your bad month. A business is several unrelated edges: a trend follower, a mean-reverter, an asset-class rotator (the across-assets table is that strategy's raw material). The measure that matters is the correlation matrix between your strategies' returns — same math as the stock co-movement graph, applied to your own algos. Low or negative correlations mean one strategy's drawdown lands during another's gain, and the combined curve smooths. When two strategies correlate highly, retire one: it's the same bet wearing two hats.

Stage 4 — Sizing and survival

Position size kills more accounts than bad entries. The rules that keep you alive: risk on the order of 1% per trade; a written drawdown budget per strategy (at what loss does it get benched — decided before deployment, not during the bleed); and the understanding that drawdowns are the fee the game charges — a strategy optimized until its backtest shows none is a curve-fit fairy tale, not a discovery. Overfitting checks are non-negotiable: out-of-sample data, walk-forward, and parameter sensitivity (if the edge only works at exactly lookback=14, there is no edge).

Stage 5 — Run the research loop forever

Every edge decays — competition arrives, costs catch up, the market regime that fed it ends. So the terminal skill isn't any strategy; it's the loop: idea → encode as a graph edge → walk-forward test → deploy small → monitor → retire → repeat. You already own the infrastructure for this loop; stage 5 is just running it on a schedule. Expect the honest timeline: 3–4 years of samples before you truly know you're good. One great quarter proves nothing — judge the process, not the outcome, because a good trade can lose and a bad trade can win, and the most dangerous event in trading is a bad process that got paid.

The realities nobody sells

  • Taxes eat active trading. US short-term gains are taxed as income — a strategy must beat buy-and-hold after that drag. Monthly cadence and long holding periods are tax strategy, not just risk strategy.
  • Costs and slippage are the house edge against you. Every trade pays the spread. High-turnover ideas that backtest beautifully often die by a thousand 3-cent cuts.
  • Individuals can't win the speed game. Intraday and HFT belong to firms with microwave towers. The individual's edges live at daily-to-monthly horizons, in patience, in taking risk others must shed, and in niches too small for funds to bother with.
  • Comfort and returns are inversely related. Extra return is payment for holding volatility others refuse. If a strategy feels perfectly comfortable, it's probably priced like bonds.
  • The field improves every year. Standing still is falling behind — which is why the research loop, not any strategy, is the asset.

Your stack (researched July 2026)

The sobering anchor: a 2025 study found 58% of retail algo strategies collapse within three months of going live — almost always overfitting plus the execution gap, both preventable. The stack below is chosen to prevent them:

  • Broker/API: Alpaca (adopt). Commission-free US stocks/ETFs via API with unlimited, high-fidelity paper trading and no minimum — the standard individual algo on-ramp in 2026. Interactive Brokers later, only if you outgrow ETFs into futures/FX. The Robinhood agentic cash account stays for tiny live validation, not the pipeline.
  • Books, in order: Ernest Chan, Quantitative Trading (the mechanics and honesty of running a one-person quant shop) → Stefan Jansen, Machine Learning for Algorithmic Trading 2nd ed. (the full modern idea→backtest workflow; skip the deep-learning chapters for now) → QuantStart's free backtesting-pitfalls series as connective tissue.
  • Cost discipline: model commissions + spread + slippage in every backtest and require the edge to survive at double your cost assumption. Real fills run ~1.5 ticks worse than the signal — enough to erase a fifth of a thin edge.
  • Tax discipline: short-term gains are taxed as income (up to ~37%) vs 15–20% long-term — the single strongest argument for the monthly, low-turnover cadence this whole system already uses.

The 5-step path from here to live: (1) add the cost model to the harness and re-verify every edge survives 2× costs; (2) paper-trade the automated loop on Alpaca for a full quarter, zero manual touches; (3) compare paper vs backtest — material divergence means overfit or mis-costed, fix before funding; (4) fund a $1–5k sleeve with hard-coded position caps and a drawdown kill-switch; (5) scale only after months of live matching expectations, keeping turnover monthly for the tax man.

The frontier (updated July 2026)

What the newest research says, and why this system teaches the old stuff anyway:

  • Alpha decays hyperbolically, and it's measured now. Roughly half of a published strategy's excess return vanishes after publication as the crowd arrives, and 2026 work models the decay curve explicitly — with crowding accelerating since 2015. Crucially, "mechanical" signals (like momentum: buy recent winners) crowd fastest, while "judgment" signals decay slower. Lesson: the newest published strategy is often the most crowded thing you can buy.
  • Durability is regime-conditional — the frontier now scores strategies by their worst-regime performance rather than their average, which is precisely the discipline this site's graph applies to every edge.
  • The LLM-agent wave is real but young. 2025–26 produced a burst of frameworks where LLM agents generate and test alpha factors in loops with anti-overfitting regularization (QuantAgent, AlphaAgent, R&D-Agent at NeurIPS 2025, TradingAgents, and KDD'26's AlphaForgeBench benchmark). Three years into the LLM era, the industry verdict is "transformative for research throughput, oversold for direct trading decisions." What you're building — an agent that researches, tests against a memory graph, and proposes — is the current architecture of the field, at personal scale.
  • Why the canonical families still come first: trend, momentum, and mean reversion have survived decades post-publication because they're risk premia — payment for discomfort the crowd structurally refuses to hold — not clever mispricings that arbitrage away. For an individual account, durable beats novel, every time. Learn the six families deeply; treat each shiny new paper as a hypothesis for the graph, not a strategy for your money.
The five-line summary

Explainable edge → deploy small and mediocre → diversify across uncorrelated edges → size to survive → research forever. Every stage has a kill-criterion written in advance. That's the entire profession; everything else is decoration.
