How to watch this

Blue is buy & hold; olive is the trend+regime strategy; the strip under the chart shows the three signals each month (top row trend, middle credit, bottom vol regime — red means warning). Watch 2008: the signals stack, the strategy steps aside, and the blue line falls twice as far as the olive one. Then watch 2010–2019: repeated false alarms where de-risking cost a little upside each time. That's the honest shape of the trade — insurance premiums are paid in good years, the payout comes in the terrible ones. The tally cards keep the running score so you can judge whether the insurance was worth it at any point in history.

What this means for your money

  • The tally is an insurance ledger, and you now know the premium. "False alarms" is the premium — upside sacrificed in months the warning was wrong. "Storms dodged" is the payout — losses avoided in months it was right. You are choosing whether to own this insurance policy. Most people buy it accidentally (by panic-selling at the bottom, the world's most expensive premium) or refuse it accidentally (by never having a plan). This page lets you choose it deliberately, knowing the historical price.
  • Why halving the drawdown matters more than it looks: losses are asymmetric. Fall 17% and you need +20% to get even. Fall 46% and you need +85%. Deep holes cost years, and if you ever need the money during one — house, emergency — the loss becomes permanent. That asymmetry is why every professional risk desk on earth manages drawdown first and return second.
  • Expect roughly two false alarms per real storm — that's the normal ratio, not a broken system. Watch the replay: the rule cried wolf in 2010, 2011, 2015, 2018… and paid for everything in 2008 and 2022. When a warning fires and nothing happens, the correct response is "premium paid," not "the system doesn't work." Deciding that in advance is what keeps you following the plan on the day it matters.
  • The concrete practice for your account: your VOO core tracks the blue line. Once a month — month-end only, never intraday — check the dashboard. Three green lights: do nothing. Trend breaks: cut equity exposure to half. Two warnings: defensive. Signals clear: step back in, mechanically, even if it feels wrong (re-entry after a false alarm always feels wrong — the replay shows it was right to re-enter every single time).
  • What this replay can't tell you: whether the next storm looks like the last ones. Monthly signals half-dodged 2020's crash because it took three weeks, not three quarters. The system is built for slow-motion bear markets (2000, 2008, 2022), which are historically where the catastrophic losses live — flash crashes are the deductible you still pay.
